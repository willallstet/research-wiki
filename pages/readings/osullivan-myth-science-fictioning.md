# Myth-Science and the Fictioning of Reality

**By:** Simon O'Sullivan · **Published:** *Paragrana* 25 (2016), no. 2, De Gruyter,
pp. 80–93

An essay tracked for [[ai-and-machine-vision]] (the algorithmic sublime and
post-internet art) and [[archives-and-wikipedia]] (the "archival turn" it critiques
and online image-worlds). It shares the wiki's hyperstition thread directly:
O'Sullivan glosses [[ccru-writings-1997-2003]]'s central concept and builds on it.
Read in full from the local PDF.

## What it argues

O'Sullivan proposes contemporary art practice as a form of **myth-science**,
defined as a **"fictioning" of reality** — "the imaginative transformation of the
world through fiction," or *mythopoeisis*
(`db61878f6644a0a799ed3a505aaae0ab.pdf:6`). The term is borrowed from Sun Ra and
Afrofuturism (via Kodwo Eshun) and from Mike Kelley (`…pdf:1` n.1). Much of it is
developed through his collaborative "performance fiction" **Plastique Fantastique**
(with David Burrows).

Art that "engages directly with the world as-it-is … already surrenders some of
its power" — even institutional critique is "trapped by its target" and becomes "a
kind of melancholic echo chamber" (`…pdf:2`). The **"archival turn"** in
contemporary art is a "softer example of this logic": it "gathers together hitherto
separate elements under a banner … but, crucially, it does not necessarily
transform these elements," offering "nothing more than a product … designed to meet
the desire for knowledge" of the world as-it-is (`…pdf:2`). Against this, art can be
"specifically other-worldly without meaning it is somehow outside the world";
following Deleuze, it is "future-orientated," addressed to a people "yet-to-come"
(`…pdf:2`–3).

## Fictioning: synchronic and diachronic

O'Sullivan splits fictioning into two operations (`…pdf:4`–5):

1. **Synchronic** — the production of "untimely images … that speak back to their
   producer." A "magical function": "the summoning forth of something hitherto
   unknown and unseen." The goal is "to extract a certain objectness (something
   non-human) from an all-too-human subject."
2. **Diachronic** — "time itself becomes a material": motifs (in Plastique
   Fantastique's case, avatars) "appear, disappear then reappear," so a practice is
   "a palimpsest even when it looks relatively simple" — "a backward-hurled fragment
   of a future world."

Both are a **layering** (spatial, then temporal) producing a "spatio-temporal
density" — "a different space-time" that constitutes art "rather than simply the
production of a commodity," a "counter-sorcery pitched against the phantasmagoria of
the commodity" (`…pdf:6`).

## The hyperstition connection

O'Sullivan explicitly links fictioning to **hyperstition** as developed by Nick
Land and the Ccru: he cites Alex Williams's gloss — hyperstition as "narratives able
to effectuate their own reality through the workings of feedback loops, generating
new sociopolitical attractors" — and argues fictioning "involves both a narrative
function … and 'sociopolitical attractors' (untimely images)" (`…pdf:7` n.5). This
is the same self-fulfilling-fiction mechanism the wiki tracks via
[[ccru-writings-1997-2003]] ("an element of effective culture that makes itself
real").

## Post-internet art and the colonized unconscious

Fictioning "dovetails with the idea of post-internet art, or art that is made from
and for the web of images that now doubles — and troubles — our own world of
things" (`…pdf:7`). These "collapsing worlds … are already in contact and
'communication' with image-worlds that are increasingly not of human generation"
(`…pdf:7`) — a striking anticipation of machine-generated image ecologies. In the
conclusion, "Capital … has increasingly co-opted even our dream worlds": Facebook,
Twitter and "all the other filtering super-nodes of a once wild … web offer up a
restricted repository of images … often just more-of-the-same," so "we become the
spectators of our own subjection" (`…pdf:9`). Social media "filters our experiences,
flattens subjective modellings." Fictioning is offered as a **counter**: "the
reclaiming and unleashing of this unconscious," producing "new myths and new dream
worlds" (`…pdf:9`).

## Coda: Guattari's fabulous images

The essay closes on Guattari's "fabulous images" (from *Schizoanalytic
Cartographies*): art as "a kind of subjective technology … involved in the
production of a different mode of being in the world" (`…pdf:11`). Guattari's
example is Genet's fascination with the Black Panthers, whose "ways of being and
dressing … almost overnight change the way black people as a whole perceive the
colour of their skin" (`…pdf:11`). Fabulous images act as "probe-heads" and
"existential operators," and art becomes "a technology of the self" pitched against
what Burroughs and Deleuze called **Control** (`…pdf:12`).

## What it is

An art-theory essay that defines contemporary art practice as a **myth-science**
— "a kind of fictioning of reality" (`Myth-Science.pdf:p1 @n/a`). The term
*myth-science* is "borrowed from Sun Ra and Afrofuturism more generally"
(`Myth-Science.pdf:p1n1 @n/a`); much of the argument is drawn from O'Sullivan's
own collaborative "performance fiction" *Plastique Fantastique* (with David
Burrows). The essay is heavily Deleuzo-Guattarian and closes with a coda on
Guattari's "fabulous images."

## Its central idea: fictioning

Art's power, for O'Sullivan, lies in being "of the world but not of the
(dominant) code of that world" — being, "in this sense, occult"
(`Myth-Science.pdf:p3 @n/a`). Against the "archival turn" (which merely "gathers
together hitherto separate elements" without transforming them) and against
"critical" art trapped by mirroring its target, he wants art that is "untimely" —
"future-orientated," addressed to a people or audience "yet-to-come"
(`Myth-Science.pdf:p2–3 @n/a`).

**Fictioning** works along two axes:

- **Synchronic** — "the production of untimely images … that speak back to their
  producer," extracting "a certain objectness (something non-human) from an
  all-too-human subject" (`Myth-Science.pdf:p4–5 @n/a`).
- **Diachronic** — "the layering of motifs to produce an accretion," so that "time
  itself becomes a material"; a practice becomes "a backward-hurled fragment of a
  future world" (`Myth-Science.pdf:p5 @n/a`).

The payoff is **mythopoeisis**: "the imaginative transformation of the world
through fiction," where "it is no longer clear where the fiction itself ends and
so-called reality begins" (`Myth-Science.pdf:p6–7 @n/a`). The final section reads
this as "reclaiming the unconscious" against a Capital that has "co-opted even our
dream worlds," and against "Web 2.0 and its logics" — "Facebook and Twitter and
all the other filtering super-nodes of a once wild … web" that "flatten
subjective modellings" (`Myth-Science.pdf:p9 @n/a`).

## Its explicit link to hyperstition

O'Sullivan directly ties fictioning to **[[ccru-writings-1997-2003|hyperstition]]**:
he invokes "the concept of 'hyperstition' as developed by Nick Land (and the
Cybernetic Culture Research Unit (Ccru))" as pointing "likewise … to elements of
fiction making themselves real," citing Alex Williams's gloss of hyperstitions as
"narratives able to effectuate their own reality through the workings of feedback
loops, generating new sociopolitical attractors" (`Myth-Science.pdf:p7n5 @n/a`).
This makes the essay the natural bridge between the wiki's Ccru material and its
art-practice concerns — see [[fictioning-and-hyperstition]].

## Why it's here

- **Concept fit:** [[fictioning-and-hyperstition]] — this is, with
  [[ccru-writings-1997-2003]], one of the two anchor texts of that concept:
  Ccru supplies the term *hyperstition*; O'Sullivan supplies the art-practice
  articulation (*fictioning* / *myth-science*) and explicitly connects the two.
- **Concept fit:** [[ai-and-machine-vision]] / [[archives-and-wikipedia]] — the

  @n/a`) and its critique of social-media image-banks map directly onto

  Are.na vector search (`index.html:74`), and the machine-indexed image long tail

## Assessment

- **A primary art-theory artifact**, read on its own terms. Its claims are
  programmatic and generative (a manifesto for a *practice*), not empirical; the
  value is in the vocabulary it hands the wiki — *fictioning*, *myth-science*,
  *untimely images*, the *synchronic / diachronic* split — and in how cleanly it
  stitches Ccru's hyperstition to a working account of what post-internet art
  *does*.
- **High leverage, dense idiom.** Thoroughly Deleuze/Guattari-inflected
  (deterritorialization, minor literature, the war machine, Guattari's Z-Point
  and "fabulous images"), so it rewards a reader already oriented in that
  tradition. The keystone secondary text for [[fictioning-and-hyperstition]].
