# Ccru: Writings 1997–2003

**By:** The Cybernetic Culture Research Unit (Ccru) · **Published:** Time Spiral
Press, 2015 (collecting texts written 1997–2003) ·
[read the PDF](https://files.libcom.org/files/%5BCcru,_Nick_Land%5D_Ccru_Writings_1997-2003(BookZZ.org).pdf)

A reading tracked for [[ai-and-machine-vision]] (the algorithmic sublime),
[[media-infrastructure]], and [[archives-and-wikipedia]].

## What it is

The complete collected texts of the Ccru, the late-1990s theory-fiction
collective that orbited the University of Warwick's philosophy department (Nick
Land, Sadie Plant, and others), assembled here after the group's website "flickered
in and out of existence" and finally vanished — the loss of that "principal Ccru
archival deposit" is what "prompted the present publication" (foreword). The
foreword calls the book "sheer documentation," expected "not to clarify anything,
but much rather the reverse," and notes that "factual errors, conceptual
absurdities, and ethico-political monstrosities have been left undisturbed"
(foreword).

The material is organised into eight thematic parts — *Id(entity)*, *Cthulhu
Club*, *AOE/Axsys*, *Black Atlantis*, *Barker*, *Sarkon*, *Cybergothic*,
*Pandemonium* — plus a **Glossary** and an appendix on TV demonism (table of
contents, `Ccru Writings 1997-2003.pdf:11157–11232 @n/a`). It mixes manifestos,
fake ethnography, invented mythos (the drowned continent of Lemuria, the Nma
tribes, the Old Ones), and a numerological system, the **Numogram** — "a
diagrammatization of decimal numeracy, providing the key to Lemurian culture
(demonism and time sorcery)," consisting of ten interconnected zones
(`Ccru Writings 1997-2003.pdf:10775–10777 @n/a`).

## Its central idea: hyperstition

The book's most durable export is the concept of **hyperstition** — glossed as an
"element of effective culture that makes itself real, through fictional quantities
functioning as time-travelling potentials … a coincidence intensifier"
(`Ccru Writings 1997-2003.pdf:10627–10629 @n/a`). Elsewhere: "Hype, hyping,
hyperpropagation belongs to a strain of time-warp cybernetic fiction that cannot be
judged true or false because it makes itself real"
(`Ccru Writings 1997-2003.pdf:6226–6228 @n/a`). Ccru itself is defined, in its own
glossary, as a "cryptic hyperstitional entity, with apparent Neolemurian
tendencies, involved in the scripting of various microcultural transmutations"
(`Ccru Writings 1997-2003.pdf:10443–10444 @n/a`) — a collective that "has no
genealogy, geographical centre, biographical attribution, or institutional
dependency" (`Ccru Writings 1997-2003.pdf:98–100 @n/a`).

The texts read the late-90s dot-com boom through this lens: "Cyber-hype libidinally
invests its own semiotic, propagating fictional quantities … making itself up as it
goes along," and Alan Greenspan's "irrational exuberance" is set beside the
"Lemurian Necronomicon" as two names for the same self-fulfilling market delirium
(`Ccru Writings 1997-2003.pdf:314–320 @n/a`).

## Why it's here

- **Concept fit:** [[ai-and-machine-vision]] — Ccru is a foundational reference for
  the **algorithmic sublime** thread ([[will-allstetter]]'s *Syntax* essay "Are You
  There God? It's Me, The Algorithm"): the treatment of computation as a
  quasi-religious force that "makes itself real" prefigures how awe and belief get
  projected onto opaque systems.
- **Concept fit:** [[archives-and-wikipedia]] — the book *is* a study in online
  myth-making and lossy archives: a body of collective fiction propagated through a
  website that then decayed, salvaged into print. Its "Crypt … buried deep within
  the primal sediment of the infoplex" is the net-as-mythology
  (`Ccru Writings 1997-2003.pdf:6206–6209 @n/a`).
- **Concept fit:** [[media-infrastructure]] — Cybergothic imagines the physical
  substrate of the net ("submarine … abysmal bass," the "Crypt … beneath and
  between the net") as a haunted material medium, resonant with the wiki's
  physical-internet-as-medium readings ([[sea-monsters-as-medium]]).

## Assessment

- **A primary artifact, read on its own terms.** This is theory-*fiction*, and its
  refusal of factual accuracy is the point, not a flaw — the foreword preserves its
  own "factual errors, conceptual absurdities, and ethico-political monstrosities"
  deliberately (`Ccru Writings 1997-2003.pdf:40–42 @n/a`). It is a first-order
  document of 1990s accelerationist / cyber-theory culture: valued for what it
  *does* (its mythos, rhetoric, and hyperstitional method), not for what it reports.
- **Highly generative for this wiki.** The ur-text for hyperstition and a recurring
  touchstone in exactly the post-internet / algorithmic-sublime discourse the
  concept pages track. Dense, deliberately obscure, and long (~200 pp.), so it
  rewards targeted reading around the Numogram, the Glossary, and the Cybergothic
  material over cover-to-cover.
