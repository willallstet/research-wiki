# In Free Fall: A Thought Experiment on Vertical Perspective

**By:** Hito Steyerl · **Published:** *e-flux journal* #24 (April 2011) ·
[read the essay](https://www.e-flux.com/journal/24/67860/in-free-fall-a-thought-experiment-on-vertical-perspective)
· read in full from the e-flux source.

A reading tracked for [[ai-and-machine-vision]] (machine and aerial ways of
seeing — Google Maps, satellite, drone) and [[media-infrastructure]] (the
"military-surveillance-entertainment complex" and the hardware of vertical
vision). The primary text the wiki's post-internet / Steyerl-orbit readings orbit.

## What it is

A thought experiment that opens: **"Imagine you are falling. But there is no
ground."** (`steyerl-in-free-fall.html:¶1 @n/a`). Steyerl argues that
contemporary visual culture is in a condition of **groundlessness** — a
"permanent, or at least intermittent state of free fall for subjects and objects
alike" (`steyerl-in-free-fall.html:¶3 @n/a`) — and that the stable horizon line
which underwrote linear perspective has given way to aerial and vertical views
that only *simulate* a stable ground for a distanced, superior spectator.

## The argument

- **Loss of the horizon.** Traditional orientation, and "with it, modern concepts
  of time and space," rest on "a stable line: the horizon line," whose stability
  "hinges on the stability of an observer … on a ground … that can be imagined as
  stable, even if in fact it is not" (`steyerl-in-free-fall.html:¶8 @n/a`).
  Navigation instruments (astrolabe, quadrant, sextant) refined horizon-sighting;
  "artificial horizons were eventually invented in order to create the illusion
  of stability" (`steyerl-in-free-fall.html:¶11 @n/a`).
- **Linear perspective as abstraction and power.** Following Alhazen's *Kitab
  al-Manazir* (c. 1028) and its European afterlife, perspective culminates in a
  single vanishing point (Uccello vs. the multiple vanishing points still in
  Duccio). Per **Panofsky**, it "declares the view of a one-eyed and immobile
  spectator as a norm," is "based on an abstraction, and does not correspond to
  any subjective perception," yet "computes a mathematical, flattened, infinite,
  continuous, and homogenous space, and declares it to be reality"
  (`steyerl-in-free-fall.html:¶16 @n/a`). It introduces "a linear time, which
  allows mathematical prediction and, with it, linear progress" — invoking
  **Benjamin**'s homogenous, empty time (`steyerl-in-free-fall.html:¶17 @n/a`;
  cf. [[benjamin-work-of-art-mechanical-reproduction]]).
- **Complicity in colonialism and racial propaganda.** Perspective was "an
  additional toolkit for enabling Western dominance"; in Uccello's *Miracle of the
  Desecrated Host* (1465–69) a Jewish merchant is tied to a pillar "on which
  parallels converge as if it were a target mark," the panels prefiguring the 1492
  expulsions and Columbus — "linear perspective becomes a matrix for racial and
  religious propaganda, and related atrocities" (`steyerl-in-free-fall.html:¶21
  @n/a`).
- **19th-c. destabilization — Turner.** In *The Slave Ship* (1840) "the horizon
  line, if distinguishable at all, is tilted, curved, and troubled. The observer
  has lost his stable position"; at the sight of colonialism's atrocity "linear
  perspective … is abandoned and starts tumbling and tilting"
  (`steyerl-in-free-fall.html:¶24 @n/a`). *Rain, Steam, and Speed* (1844) leaves
  the spectator "suspended in the mist, floating over an absent ground"
  (`steyerl-in-free-fall.html:¶26 @n/a`).
- **The vertical / aerial turn.** Today "aerial views: overviews, Google Map
  views, satellite views" — "what used to be called a God's-eye view" — supplement
  and replace linear perspective (`steyerl-in-free-fall.html:¶6 @n/a`). Drones
  "survey, track, and kill"; 3D cinema stages "vertiginous flights into abysses"
  within Elsaesser's **military-surveillance-entertainment complex**
  (`steyerl-in-free-fall.html:¶30 @n/a`). Steyerl cites **Weizman**'s "vertical
  sovereignty" (power splitting space into "stacked horizontal layers") and
  **Mbembe**'s necropolitics ("most of the policing is done from the air")
  (`steyerl-in-free-fall.html:¶32–33 @n/a`). The crucial reversal: these views
  "do not actually portray a stable ground … [they] create a supposition that it
  exists," so "the perspective from above establish[es] an imaginary floating
  observer and an imaginary stable ground" — "a radicalization … of the paradigm
  of linear perspective" (`steyerl-in-free-fall.html:¶34–35 @n/a`).
- **The Adorno turn — terror and liberation.** Falling is also "a new
  representational freedom." Via Adorno's *Negative Dialectics* ("The vertigo which
  this causes is an *index veri*"), Steyerl reframes groundlessness: "Falling is
  corruption as well as liberation." The essay ends on "a social and political
  dreamscape of radicalized class war from above" — but also "a new certainty
  falling into place," a place that "promises no community, but a shifting
  formation" (`steyerl-in-free-fall.html:¶38–40 @n/a`).

## Why it's here

- **Concept fit:** [[ai-and-machine-vision]] — the essay's account of the
  disembodied, "remote-controlled gaze, outsourced to machines and other objects"
  (`steyerl-in-free-fall.html:¶35 @n/a`) is the theoretical frame for machine and
  aerial vision, adjacent to [[will-allstetter]]'s machine-vision and
  image-search work. The critique of perspective-as-abstraction pairs with
  [[chirimuuta-reflex-machine-cybernetic-brain]]'s critique of scientific
  abstraction, from a different (visual-political) angle.
- **Concept fit:** [[media-infrastructure]] — 3D and aerial vision "intensif[y]
  hierarchies of material required to access this new visuality," producing "new
  markets for hardware and software" (`steyerl-in-free-fall.html:¶30 @n/a`): the
  hardware substrate behind the image, squarely the wiki's infrastructure thread.
- **Lineage:** cites [[benjamin-work-of-art-mechanical-reproduction|Benjamin]] on
  homogenous empty time; shares the post-internet / net-art milieu of
  [[osullivan-myth-science-fictioning]] and [[sea-monsters-as-medium]].

## Assessment

- **A primary artifact of contemporary art theory**, read on its own terms.
  Steyerl's "thought experiment" is an *argument-image*, not an empirical study;
  its readings of Uccello, Panofsky, and Turner are interpretive and rhetorical
  (the point is what the reading *does*, not art-historical fact-checking).
- **A hub text for this wiki.** It gathers perspective, colonialism,
  surveillance, drone/aerial vision, and the politics of the image into one
  essay, and links laterally to Benjamin, Chirimuuta, and the post-internet
  cluster — the primary node for the "vertical / aerial vision" strand of
  [[ai-and-machine-vision]].
