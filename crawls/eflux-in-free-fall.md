## Pages read (5)

### The History of the Sextant — Peter Ifland
http://www.mat.uc.pt/~helios/Mestre/Novemb00/H61iflan.htm
*Linked as: "→" (footnote 2, on early navigation)*

Traces celestial-navigation instruments from Arab tools (the kamal) through quadrant, astrolabe, and cross-staff to the sextant, centering on John Hadley's 1731 doubly-reflecting octant as the basis of the modern sextant. This is the ground of Steyerl's "brief history of the horizon" — how the horizon line became a measurable instrument of orientation (and, she argues, of colonial navigation).

### On the Concept of History ("Theses on the Philosophy of History") — Walter Benjamin (1940)
http://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/benjamin/1940/history.htm
*Linked as: "→" (footnote 4, on homogeneous empty time)*

Benjamin's 18 theses critiquing linear, progressive history through a Marxist-messianic lens — home of the "Angel of History" driven backward into the future by the storm of progress, and "no document of culture which is not simultaneously one of barbarism." Steyerl borrows Benjamin's "homogeneous, empty time" to argue linear perspective spatializes *and temporalizes* the same calculable, progressive worldview.

### Necropolitics — Achille Mbembe (Public Culture 15.1, 2003) [PDF]
https://sites.duke.edu/writing_is_thinking_f21/files/2021/10/Mbembe_Necropolitics.pdf
*Linked as: "→" (footnote 8, quoted directly)*

Mbembe's foundational essay on sovereignty as the power to dictate who may live and who must die (extending Foucault's biopolitics). Steyerl quotes it directly on aerial policing — "occupation of the skies… most of the policing is done from the air" (UAVs, reconnaissance jets, Hawkeyes, satellites, "hologrammatization") — to tie the vertical/aerial gaze to necropower. (PDF fetched; body is scanned/encoded but the source and relevance are confirmed.)

### (Jena Revisited) Ten Tentative Tenets — Dieter Roelstraete (e-flux journal #16, May 2010)
https://www.e-flux.com/journal/16/61284/jena-revisited-ten-tentative-tenets/
*Linked as: "→" (footnote 9, on the "new visual normality")*

An e-flux journal essay by curator Dieter Roelstraete, cited (alongside Jennifer Allen's "That Eye, The Sky") as describing the "new normality" of surveillance-saturated subjectivity that Steyerl says the view-from-above installs.

### Is a Museum a Factory? — Hito Steyerl (e-flux journal #7, June 2009)
https://www.e-flux.com/journal/07/61390/is-a-museum-a-factory/
*Linked as: "→" (footnote 14, self-citation)*

Steyerl's own earlier e-flux essay, on the museum-as-factory and the "multiple spectator" of dispersed multiscreen projection. She cites it for the point that new projection spaces suppose no single unified horizon but "a multiple spectator… created and recreated by ever-new articulations of the crowd." Useful as an entry to the broader Steyerl corpus on the site.

## Takeaway

The article's outbound links split cleanly in two: ~35 e-flux platform chrome, and **8 footnote citations that are the essay's actual intellectual scaffolding**. The live ones form a tight, coherent bibliography of Steyerl's argument — navigation/the horizon (Ifland's sextant), linear time (Benjamin), aerial necropower (Mbembe), the surveillance "new normal" (Roelstraete), and her own corpus (*Is a Museum a Factory?*). Three key citations have **rotted** — most notably Elsaesser's 3-D essay, which Steyerl names as the paper's "blueprint," and Weizman's "Politics of Verticality," her source for vertical sovereignty. The crawl is therefore both a reading aid and a **link-rot record**: if a concept page cites Steyerl's Weizman/Elsaesser sources, it should pin to an archived copy rather than these dead permalinks. Two of the live citations (Roelstraete, Steyerl's own #7) are themselves e-flux journal essays worth a separate crawl if the Steyerl/post-internet concept cluster grows.
